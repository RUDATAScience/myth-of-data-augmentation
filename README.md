# Oversampling Paradox Simulator: The Self-Contradiction of Data Augmentation in Distorted Data

[![Python](https://img.shields.io/badge/Python-3.8%2B-blue.svg)](https://www.python.org/)
[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-yellow.svg)](https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT)

This repository is a Python simulation that proves how **oversampling (such as SMOTE or simple duplication)—a standard remedy for imbalanced data—is both meaningless and harmful** when "social desirability bias" (peer pressure or conformity) occurs in surveys and large-scale data collection.

It quantifies the process by which the common sense in the machine learning field—"if data is lacking, just inflate it"—degrades into the "deification of pure noise" in systems where the information generation process is distorted.

## 📌 Background

In data analysis, when the voices of an extreme minority (e.g., "Rating 1") are scarce, practitioners often attempt to improve AI model training accuracy by imputing and amplifying the data.

However, this simulation proves the following cruel reality (the Oversampling Paradox):

1. **Fugitives**: In environments with strong peer pressure, the vast majority of the true minority who originally should have given a "Rating 1" have already succumbed to conformity and hidden themselves (fled) into "Rating 3" or "Rating 4".
2. **Signal-to-Noise Ratio Reversal (SNR < 1)**: The few "Rating 1"s remaining in the data after conformity are a mixture of the small number of people who stayed true to their beliefs and "pure noise" (such as response errors) generated randomly by the majority. In many cases, the noise outnumbers the true signals.
3. **Algorithmic Pollution**: No matter how much you inflate Rating 1 in this state, it does not restore the "missing dissatisfied demographic." It merely amplifies noise and extreme opinions, causing the worst kind of algorithmic pollution by forcing the AI to learn a "fake minority profile" that does not exist in reality.

## 🧮 Mathematical Model

An individual's final utility ($U_{\text{total}}$) is defined as a linear combination of their intrinsic "true intention" ($U_{\text{true}}$) and extrinsic "conformity" ($U_{\text{target}}$). The selection probability is then calculated through a Softmax function.

$$U_{\text{total}} = (1 - v_2) U_{\text{true}} + v_2 U_{\text{target}}$$

* **$v_2$**: The weight of social desirability (conformity). Set to the critical point of 0.5 in this experiment.
* **$\beta$**: The respondent's confidence level.
* **Simulation Scale**: Out of 10,000 total participants, the true minority is set to 10% (1,000 people), and the response behavior after conformity is reproduced using the Monte Carlo method.
